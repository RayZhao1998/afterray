# AfterRay icon concepts

Generated with the built-in image generation tool on 2026-08-12. The Raycast
homepage screenshot was used only as a mood and rendering reference; the marks
are original and avoid Raycast's stacked-diamond geometry.

## 1. Photon Slash

![Photon Slash](afterray-photon-slash.png)

The simplest direction: one diagonal scarlet ray cutting through black space.
It reads clearly at small sizes, but the concept is less ownable than the other
directions.

Prompt:

> Design a square macOS app icon for AfterRay with exactly one dominant
> diagonal scarlet light ray cutting through deep black space. Give the ray a
> small white-hot core, controlled crimson afterglow, fine analog grain, and a
> premium cinematic 3D finish. Keep a bold, simple silhouette readable at 32
> px. No text, letters, rounded-corner mask, border, mockup, generic lightning,
> multiple stripes, or copied Raycast geometry.

## 2. Temporal Aperture

![Temporal Aperture](afterray-temporal-aperture.png)

A single ray passes through an obsidian temporal aperture. This direction best
connects the icon to AfterRay's capture and recall timeline.

Prompt:

> Design a square macOS app icon for AfterRay around memory and time. A single
> horizontal red ray passes through a thick minimal black circular aperture,
> subtly deforming it like a time ripple and leaving one fading afterimage.
> Render it as cinematic abstract 3D with matte obsidian, scarlet volumetric
> light, controlled grain, and a simple silhouette readable at 32 px. No text,
> literal clock, letters, rounded-corner mask, multiple rays, neon clutter, or
> copied Raycast geometry.

## 3. Afterimage A

![Afterimage A](afterray-afterimage-a.png)

A diagonal ray and an obsidian wedge imply an A through negative space. This
direction has the strongest standalone brand recognition.

Prompt:

> Design a square macOS app icon for AfterRay whose abstract negative space
> subtly suggests a capital A. One scarlet ray rises diagonally through a dark
> monolithic obsidian form, while a soft delayed red afterimage completes the
> suggestion without drawing a literal letter. Make it sculptural, sharp,
> cinematic, and readable at 16–32 px. No text, wordmark, rounded-corner mask,
> warning-sign styling, gaming-logo styling, multiple ribbons, or copied
> Raycast geometry.

## 4. Prism Core

![Prism Core](afterray-prism-core.png)

A narrow ray enters a dark prism and exits as a broad afterglow. It expresses
the name most literally and works well as cinematic product art.

Prompt:

> Design a square macOS app icon for AfterRay showing one narrow red light ray
> entering a compact floating black obsidian prism and emerging as a broad soft
> red afterglow. Use large shapes, rich negative space, physically plausible
> light, controlled bloom, and a premium cinematic 3D finish readable at 32 px.
> No rainbow refraction, text, letters, rounded-corner mask, border, mockup,
> surrounding scene, lens-flare clutter, or copied Raycast geometry.

## Recommendation

Start from **Temporal Aperture** for product meaning, or **Afterimage A** for a
more ownable long-term brand. The next pass should simplify the chosen geometry
and tune its contrast at 16, 32, 64, and 128 px before exporting an AppIcon set.

## Black-hole iterations

These iterations use the supplied black-hole image as a reference for event
horizon lighting and gravitational lensing. They keep the palette red rather
than reproducing the photograph's warm accretion disk.

### 5. Event Horizon Axis

![Event Horizon Axis](afterray-event-horizon-axis.png)

> Evolve Temporal Aperture into an original AfterRay icon. Replace the
> mechanical ring with a simplified black-hole event horizon: a large dark core
> surrounded by one thick scarlet accretion arc. Preserve one clean horizontal
> ray; bend part of that same ray around the upper and lower rim before it
> recombines. Keep it cinematic but icon-like, readable at 16–32 px, with no
> stars, planets, orange light, text, mockup, or copied logo geometry.

### 6. Gravitational Ray

![Gravitational Ray](afterray-gravitational-ray.png)

> A single diagonal scarlet beam enters from the lower left, disappears behind
> a pure-black event horizon, and reappears at the upper right. The afterimage
> of the same beam bends into one incomplete red halo. Limit the icon to three
> major shapes, tight red bloom, and a silhouette recognizable at 16 px. Avoid
> astrophotography, stars, orange light, eye-like details, portals, and multiple
> independent rays.

### 7. Apex Event A

![Apex Event A](afterray-apex-event-a.png)

> Make the capital A immediately recognizable: one scarlet diagonal ray forms
> the left stroke, one broad obsidian blade forms the right stroke, and a curved
> gravitational-lensing arc forms the crossbar. Put a small dark event horizon
> at the apex. Use a wide base, distinct triangular counter-space, thick simple
> geometry, controlled red bloom, and no additional text, stars, or space scene.

### 8. Lensed A

![Lensed A](afterray-lensed-a.png)

> Build an unmistakable capital A from one continuous ray: it rises from the
> lower left, curves around a small event horizon at the apex, then falls as a
> wider fading afterimage to form the right leg. Add one short obsidian crossbar
> with a red edge. Center the mark, keep generous black margins, and avoid
> ribbons, warning-sign styling, multiple beams, stars, and excessive glow.

Of the black-hole directions, **Gravitational Ray** is the strongest icon. The
A iterations fix the earlier readability problem, but the circular apex can be
mistaken for a decorative dot; a future pass should integrate the event horizon
inside the A's counter-space instead.

### 9. Gravitational Ray — Wide Halo

![Gravitational Ray — Wide Halo](afterray-gravitational-ray-wide-halo.png)

> Preserve the black disk, diagonal ray, intersection points, framing, and
> red-black palette of Gravitational Ray. Make only the event-horizon glow about
> 2.5 times wider: retain a bright narrow inner rim, add a medium textured
> accretion band, and extend a broad diffuse scarlet corona outward. Keep the
> upper-right arc slightly brighter, while avoiding a uniform neon-ring look.

### 10. Gravitational Ray — Refined Circle

![Gravitational Ray — Refined Circle](afterray-gravitational-ray-refined-circle.png)

> Preserve the core diameter, center, diagonal ray, wide halo, rotational
> texture, palette, and framing. Repair only the event-horizon boundary into a
> precise, smooth circle with a narrow, optically uniform scarlet rim. At both
> ray contacts, remove bumps, notches, white spill, and thickness changes; keep
> the brighter bloom outside the circle so the black silhouette remains clean
> and uninterrupted.

### 11. Coral Orbit

![Coral Orbit](afterray-coral-orbit.png)

This is a new generation rather than an edit of a previous concept. The ray
runs behind a complete circular rim, eliminating the fragile ray-to-ring splice.
Its coral, tomato-red, watermelon, and peach highlights make the result feel
younger and more optimistic than the earlier crimson palette.

> Create a new AfterRay icon with a centered perfect black disk and a flawless,
> continuous 360-degree circular rim. Place one lower-left to upper-right ray
> behind both the disk and rim, diffusing it into a broad gravitational-lensing
> corona before it reaches the circle. Use vivid coral, tomato red, watermelon,
> and peach-pink highlights on soft charcoal. Keep the rim smooth and unbroken,
> particularly on the right, with no notch, seam, flare node, red leakage into
> the core, horror mood, or astrophotography clutter.

## Prism and possible-futures explorations

This group replaces the black-hole metaphor. One fixed, single-color past ray
reaches the present prism and becomes multiple colorful possible futures. The
output is intentionally not a conventional ordered rainbow.

### 12. Pixel Prism Burst

![Pixel Prism Burst](afterray-pixel-prism-burst.png)

A high-energy pixel-art treatment with a strong central crystal. It has the
largest visual impact, but currently carries more detail than an everyday app
icon needs.

### 13. Pixel Future Paths

![Pixel Future Paths](afterray-pixel-future-paths.png)

The prism converts a single past into three visibly different routes. This is
the clearest expression of choice and alternate futures in the pixel group.

### 14. Pixel Memory Gate

![Pixel Memory Gate](afterray-pixel-memory-gate.png)

The past enters a dark container and becomes a colorful internal mosaic. It is
distinctive, though the result reads more like a game artifact or portal.

### 15. Folded Futures

![Folded Futures](afterray-folded-futures.png)

An asymmetric glass prism unfolds one coral past ray into four broad translucent
planes. The planes suggest layers of memory and possible futures instead of a
textbook spectrum. This is the strongest modern direction in the group.

### 16. Spectrum A

![Spectrum A](afterray-spectrum-a.png)

Two crystal blades and refracted ribbons form an A-like silhouette. It has
strong brand presence, but letter-A technology marks are a crowded category.

### 17. Future Tracks

![Future Tracks](afterray-future-tracks.png)

A minimal, vector-friendly timeline symbol: one coral track enters an offset
prismatic notch and four possible tracks leave it. It survives reduction well,
but needs a more proprietary center shape to avoid reading as data routing.

### Recommendation

Continue with **Folded Futures** as the primary product-icon direction and
**Pixel Future Paths** as the expressive pixel direction. A useful next pass
would simplify each to a 16–32 px silhouette while preserving its core idea:
broad unfolding planes for Folded Futures and three visibly divergent choices
for Pixel Future Paths.

## Restrained black-and-red prism explorations

This group responds to the oversaturated RGB quality of the previous prism
set. It uses the original Raycast homepage image only as a reference for visual
hierarchy: large black negative space, scarlet as the dominant color, faint cool
accents hidden in shadows, broad light planes, and fine grain. No Raycast mark
or geometry is reproduced.

### 18. Dark Refraction

![Dark Refraction](afterray-dark-refraction.png)

Three broad red planes emerge from a dark prism. This comes closest to the
reference image's light treatment, though its triangular center belongs to a
crowded category of technology marks.

### 19. Spectral Shadow

![Spectral Shadow](afterray-spectral-shadow.png)

A single ray passes through a triangular slit in a graphite time gate and
becomes several red afterimages. It preserves the prism metaphor without
drawing a literal rainbow. This is the strongest professional direction in the
group.

### 20. Prism Fold

![Prism Fold](afterray-prism-fold.png)

Refraction happens mostly inside an interlocking graphite object, with only
three short output tabs. Its standalone silhouette is the most ownable, though
its industrial weight suggests infrastructure or hardware.

### 21. Noir Pixel Prism

![Noir Pixel Prism](afterray-noir-pixel-prism.png)

A limited-palette pixel version built from black, graphite, scarlet, ruby, and
small desaturated navy shadows. It is much more restrained than the previous
pixel group but remains an expressive secondary direction.

### Recommendation

Continue with **Spectral Shadow**. In the next iteration, make the central gate
slightly wider and shorter, reduce it to a more ownable silhouette, and test
whether three red afterimages communicate enough plurality without resembling
a generic routing symbol.
